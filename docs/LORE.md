## The premise: Civ → Alpha Centauri → schism

NWO picks up *after* a Civilization-style science victory. The frame of reference is
**Sid Meier's Alpha Centauri**: a colony world where the victors of Earth don't build a
utopia — they fracture into **ideological nations** and fight to impose rival visions of
the future. In NWO those nations are Civ5's **social-policy trees** made flesh.

The title carries the double meaning the game is built around:

> **New World Order** = the political order being fought over **on the literal new world
> (the planet)**. Every faction believes *its* doctrine is the rightful New World Order.

## Timeline

### 1. The Victory & the Exodus

A civilization on Earth won the long game through science and launched the colony ark
**Exodus** toward Alpha Centauri. Aboard were the **Founders** — and, sealed in the
ship's archive, the full accumulated knowledge of Earth.

### 2. The Cradle Years

Landfall on the planet **Cradle** succeeded. For generations there was **one unified
colony**, bound by a single shared purpose: *survive and terraform*. Under that pressure
the old Earth philosophies were merely policies — useful tools, not banners. The colony
thrived.

### 3. The Sundering

Then survival was *won*. With the existential threat gone, the shared purpose that held
the colony together dissolved — and society fractured along the **same philosophical
fault lines** that the social policies had always described. Tradition, Liberty, Honor,
Commerce, Rationalism, Exploration (and others) each hardened into a **faction**, seized
a region of Cradle, and declared its vision the only legitimate order.

This collapse is **the Sundering**. It is where every match begins.

### 4. The Long Salvage (why they fight with spears)

The Sundering didn't just split the people — it shattered the colony's **industrial base**
and **sealed the ark's archive** (the knowledge of Earth is intact but *locked*, a prize
no faction yet controls). Cut off from the machines that made them mighty, the factions
fell back on **salvage and rediscovery**.

This is the **tech-regression hook**, and it explains the unit roster: descendants of a
star-faring civilization fight with **Warriors, Archers, and Catapults** because that's
what you can build from wreckage and ore when the factories are dead. The tech tree is a
**re-climb**, not a fresh start:

```text
Salvaged-primitive  →  Recovered colony tech  →  New planetary tech
(spears, bows,         (re-opened archive:       (Cradle-native science:
 ore, beasts)           power, alloys)            railguns, drones, fauna-riders)
```

So the antiquity-era units are the *opening* of an arc that climbs back toward — and then
past — what the Founders once had. Existing units are reskins of this first tier;
later tiers go unmistakably sci-fi.

## The Reavers (the dispossessed)

Not everyone joined a faction. The **Reavers** are the descendants who rejected every
doctrine — raiders who hold the ruins, pillage the settled lands, and bow to no New World
Order. They are Cradle's "barbarians," but with a point of view: they are what the
Sundering left behind, and unlike mindless raiders they can be **bought** (see
[FACTIONS.md](FACTIONS.md) and the Commerce-leaning factions).

---

## Tone

Hard sci-fi colony frontier with the bones of a Civ game showing through: a fallen
high-tech society re-climbing toward its own past, fighting over a planet that is itself a
prize. Hopeful in premise (humanity reached the stars), grim in the present (it could not
stop fighting), and **gamey** rather than solemn — fast, readable, decisive.

---

## Out of scope (setting)

- **Religion & ideology systems.** Cut for now. The would-be religious faction
  (**Piety → "The Devout"**, a cult of the Ark) and the soft-power faction
  (**Aesthetics → "The Conservatory"**) are **shelved, planned** — see
  [FACTIONS.md](FACTIONS.md). Their lore exists; their mechanics don't yet.
- **Earth.** Earth's fate after the Exodus is deliberately left blank — the story is
  entirely on Cradle.
